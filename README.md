1. FINAL PAPER TITLE

From Agricultural Support to Climate Resilience: A Data-Driven Framework for Agricultural Investment in Zimbabwe

Do not change the title unless the evidence collected during the research makes a minor refinement genuinely necessary.

The central idea of the paper is:

Zimbabwe has historically used agricultural support mechanisms such as seed, fertiliser and other input interventions, but agricultural vulnerability is not uniform across the country. This study investigates how historical agricultural and climate data can be used to identify different vulnerability profiles and develop a framework for matching agricultural conditions with appropriate categories of climate-resilient investment.

The paper should NOT simply argue that subsidies are bad or that irrigation is always better.

The purpose is to determine what the evidence shows and then develop a data-driven framework for differentiated agricultural investment.

2. CORE RESEARCH QUESTION

The primary research question is:

How can historical agricultural and climate data be used to identify agricultural vulnerabilities and inform a differentiated framework for climate-resilient agricultural investment in Zimbabwe?

⸻

3. SECONDARY RESEARCH QUESTIONS

Address the following questions where the available evidence permits:

1. How have crop production and agricultural productivity changed over time in Zimbabwe?
2. How strongly are agricultural outcomes associated with rainfall variability?
3. Which crops and agricultural systems appear most vulnerable to drought?
4. How do agricultural conditions differ across Zimbabwe’s agro-ecological regions?
5. Does increased agricultural production necessarily correspond to increased productivity?
6. To what extent are changes in output associated with changes in cultivated/harvested area rather than yield?
7. What combinations of climate risk and agricultural performance are associated with different vulnerability profiles?
8. What types of agricultural interventions logically correspond to different vulnerability profiles?
9. How can these relationships be translated into a practical framework for agricultural investment?
10. What evidence gaps remain before particular interventions can be evaluated causally?

⸻

4. MAIN OBJECTIVE

To develop a data-driven framework for targeting climate-resilient agricultural investment in Zimbabwe based on agricultural productivity and climate vulnerability.

⸻

5. SPECIFIC OBJECTIVES

The study should pursue these objectives:

1. Analyse historical trends in agricultural production and productivity in Zimbabwe.
2. Examine the relationship between rainfall variability and agricultural performance.
3. Assess differences in agricultural and climate vulnerability across Zimbabwe’s agro-ecological regions where geographically compatible data permit.
4. Examine whether changes in production are associated with changes in productivity, harvested area, or both.
5. Identify agricultural conditions associated with greater vulnerability to drought.
6. Examine the role of crop type, agricultural system, water availability and other relevant factors in vulnerability.
7. Develop a framework linking identified agricultural conditions to categories of potential climate-resilience investment.
8. Identify evidence gaps and areas requiring further empirical research before specific interventions can be evaluated.
